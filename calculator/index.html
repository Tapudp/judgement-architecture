Judgment P&L

Where should the confidence threshold sit? Every automated judgment costs a model call, a human review when the model abstains, or an error when it acts and is wrong. This prices all three, finds the cheapest threshold, and shows what miscalibration is costing you each month. Part of Judgment Architecture — the concept is explained in Judgment Economics.

Inputs

A System One model is about $0.00002. A frontier LLM call is often 100–1,000× more.
Re-contact, refund, re-shipment, penalty — whatever a mistake really costs.

How this is calculated

Cost(τ) = N·c + N·A(τ)·r + N·(1 − A(τ))·E(τ)·e — model calls, plus human review of the share A(τ) below the threshold, plus errors among the judgments acted on automatically.

Chow threshold. C. K. Chow (1970) showed that with calibrated probabilities the cost-minimising rule is: act automatically when confidence ≥ 1 − r/e, otherwise ask a human.

Calibration interest. For every band you act on, the gap between claimed and measured accuracy produces errors you did not plan for: Σ N·share·max(0, claimed − measured)·e.

Cost of trusting confidence. The difference between the total at the Chow threshold and the total at the cheapest threshold found using measured accuracy.

Thresholds are applied at band edges. The default scenarios are illustrative, not measurements of any particular model.
