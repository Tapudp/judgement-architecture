Judgment P&L

Where should the confidence threshold sit? Every automated judgment costs a model call, a human review when the model abstains, or an error when it acts and is wrong. This prices all three, finds the cheapest threshold, and shows what miscalibration is costing you each month. Part of Judgment Architecture — the concept is explained in Judgment Economics.

How this is calculated

Cost(τ) = N·c + N·A(τ)·r + N·(1 − A(τ))·E(τ)·e — model calls, plus human review of the share A(τ) below the threshold, plus errors among the judgments acted on automatically.

Chow threshold. C. K. Chow (1970) showed that with calibrated probabilities the cost-minimising rule is: act automatically when confidence ≥ 1 − r/e, otherwise ask a human.

Calibration interest. For every band you act on, the gap between claimed and measured accuracy produces errors you did not plan for: Σ N·share·max(0, claimed − measured)·e.

Cost of trusting confidence. The difference between the total at the Chow threshold and the total at the cheapest threshold found using measured accuracy.

Thresholds are applied at band edges. Reviewed judgments are assumed to end up correct. The logistics and trade scenarios are illustrative; the two real cases below use published measurements.

Bank chatbot: which of 150 intents is this?

CLINC150 benchmark · Jev 1.13 · jev-certify, Sept 2026

What they measured

  • Overall top-1 accuracy 93.0%.
  • At a threshold of 0.831 the model routes 84.75% of queries itself, with 2.65% of those wrong.
  • The rest escalate to an LLM at $4.20 per 1,000. Total $0.79 per 1,000 instead of $4.20.

The escalated band's accuracy follows from 93.0% overall. The error cost is the one at which 0.831 is Chow-optimal. The repo reports calibration error 0.099 overall but no per-band table, so bands are treated as calibrated. The calculator reproduces their cost: model + LLM = $0.786 per 1,000.

What it teaches: every threshold is a price tag. Choosing 0.831 quietly says a misrouted customer query costs about 2.5 cents. If it really costs a frustrated customer and five minutes of an agent (say $5), the right threshold is above 0.999 — with this data, that means escalating everything. AI judge: which of two answers is better?

Carnegie Mellon, arXiv 2609.26550 · Jev with GPT-6 fallback

What they measured

  • Accuracy by confidence: 99.1% at 1.0 (n=322) · 93.9% at 0.95–0.99 (n=147) · 76.5% at 0.7–0.8 (n=85) · 47.7% below 0.6 (n=65).
  • Jev $0.044 vs GPT-6 $12.18 per 1,000 judgments.
  • Escalating below 0.9: 34% escalated, 91.3% accuracy vs GPT-6's 91.7%, at 47% of the fee.

$0.122 (ten GPT-6 calls) is the error cost at which the paper's 0.9 is Chow-optimal. Only four bands are given in the text, so the preset escalates 24% instead of the paper's 34%. Treat it as a teaching approximation, not a replication.

What it teaches: overconfidence hides at the top. The 0.95–0.99 band claims ~0.97 and delivers 0.939 — about $15 per 10,000 judgments of calibration interest. And "review" need not be a person: a bigger model is a reviewer with a price.

Retail: are these two listings the same product?

Pricogni · "The thirty-cent judge"

What they measured

  • 9,081 low-confidence product matches that nobody had looked at.
  • Two thresholds: ≥ 0.8 confirm, ≤ 0.2 refute, in between stays with a person.
  • Result: 21% confirmed, 49% refuted, 30% left for humans. Whole run: $0.32, 13 minutes.
  • 50 hand-checked: all 20 refutations held.
